## 2. Claimed but not upheld

This is where the session's own account of itself and the tree disagree — including every verdict a verification pass overturned.

**Overturned #1 — "inline examples removed."** The net diff (8 fences added, 170 removed) and the two summoners' skeleton-only fences make this look closed. It isn't: `codex-keeper.md` alone carries 102 fence lines in source and the _installed_ `web-tester.md` carries 17 literal `// Good` / `// Bad` comments — exactly the pattern the brief says constrains the model's exploration space. The session's own tracker knew this (`todo/ROADMAP.md:397`, `todo/plans/summoner-context-engineering-2026-09-03.md:905`) but the claim as recorded ("upheld") does not carry that scope qualifier anywhere it's asserted as a headline result.

**Overturned #2 — "the gate's exit-0 conditions are exhaustive."** Seven conditions were enumerated and all seven reproduce. Three more do not appear anywhere in the repo: `disableAllHooks` (an org policy that silences the gate at any scope, directly falsifying `agent-system.md:433`'s "at global scope it always runs"), the shipped binary's 8-block stop-hook cap (after which a persistently-failing gate releases the agent anyway, contradicting the "iterates rather than reporting done" promise in `compilation-pipeline.md`), and the hook timeout (which discards output and lets the turn proceed). The design doc (`todo/plans/summoner-context-engineering-2026-09-03.md:315`) dropped `stop_hook_active` handling on the stated ground that it "is NOT in the documented Stop/SubagentStop contract" — but it _is_ in the shipped binary's own hook-input schema and in Anthropic's public Agent SDK reference. The decision was made on a documentation gap, not an absence.

**Overturned #3 — "the gate composes with a declared hook rather than being silently deleted."** True in `agent-source.ts` and pinned by `compiler.test.ts`. False in the artefact a reader would actually open: `apps/www/src/content/docs/docs/reference/sub-agent-anatomy.md`, edited _this session_, still documents the withdrawn exclusive-or rule ("`hooks:` in `metadata.yaml`; **otherwise** a completion gate"). And all 13 installed agents under `~/.claude/agents` still carry `SubagentStop` on disk — they were compiled 28 minutes before `agent-source.ts`'s last edit and were never recompiled, so "run it by hand through the CLI" (CLAUDE.md's own step 4) was not done against the gate's final state.

**The 86.9% figure.** `todo/plans/claude5-brief-implementation-2026-09-03.md:151` records "15,299 → 1,998 chars, an 86.9% cut." It reproduces in **no** posture, by either `.length` or UTF-8 bytes. The real number, measured the way the guard test measures it, is 15,410 → 2,374 = **84.6%**. A second plan file records a third, different figure (82.9%) for the same measurement. Three numbers, one fact, none of them agreeing — this is exactly the failure mode the repo's own briefing contract exists to prevent ("a brief carries the command, not its result").

**"Verbatim" sample is not verbatim.** `sub-agent-anatomy.md`'s quick-start block, introduced as "the top of a compiled `web-developer.md`, verbatim," shows `hooks: {"SubagentStop":...}`. The compiler emits `Stop` (`GATE_EVENT = "Stop"` in `agent-source.ts`), confirmed by rendering the actual template. The same page separately asserts `reviewer` is "the one shipped agent that carries [isolation]" — `reviewer/metadata.yaml` carries a comment stating the opposite, citing the very ruling that produced it: _"the owner does not use worktrees (ruling 2026-09-03)."_ Zero of 18 agents declare `isolation`.

**Documentation claiming re-derivation that didn't happen.** `agent-system.md` and `compilation-pipeline.md` both advanced `last_validated` to 2026-09-03 — the bible's own contract for that stamp is "the whole document was re-derived from source on that date." Both then state the compiled body's section order **backwards** against the template they cite (`output.md` before `<critical_reminders>`; the template renders the reverse), and both omit `experimental` from every exhaustive frontmatter enumeration they carry, despite the session adding that key to the template three lines from the ones they list. A document cannot claim whole-document verification and be wrong about the order of the six things it lists in order.

**A quotation that was rewritten.** `loop-prompts-bible.md:322-326` introduces a blockquote "From `/ralph-loop:ralph-loop`:" and then gives the brief's positive-framing rewrite of it. The plugin file it names, still on this machine, says the opposite ("Do not output false promises to escape the loop... even if you think you're stuck"). Rule 1 licenses rewriting _this product's own_ generated prompts; it was applied here to words attributed to someone else's file.

## 3. Upheld, and how you know

- **The template baseline is genuinely slimmer and cleaner.** Six methodology partials collapsed into one; positional regex scans for prohibition/shouting/retired-forms return zero hits across the whole `_templates/` tree; a real vitest guard (`agent-baseline-is-slim-and-positively-framed.test.ts`) renders the template through Liquid in all three skill postures rather than scanning text, and was shown to fail against a materialised HEAD template (16-17 prohibition lines, 3,839+ B of shouted repetition) before the change — a genuine red-to-green test, not an invented category.
- **Both summoners were rewritten out of the coercive idiom entirely**, not just trimmed: `agent-summoner/critical-requirements.md` went from 14 `**(You MUST...)**` lines and a `→ STOP` block to one paragraph; `critical-reminders.md` shed its four self-verification MUSTs and its "Only proceed when you have verified all requirements are met" gate.
- **Rule 3's cache-prefix invariant holds under real measurement**, not assertion: two full renders at different versions diverge on exactly one line, 14 lines from the end of a 900+-line file, across all 18 agents, not just a sample.
- **The gate's core logic is correct**, run by hand rather than read: exits 0 with no `package.json`, exits 0 with no scripts declared, exits 2 with the checker's own stderr on a real TypeScript failure, exits 0 in this repository today (40.9s with turbo cache).
- **`isolation` and `cacheTtl` support is real end-to-end** — accepted by both Zod schemas, both JSON schemas, sanitized, emitted only when set, round-tripped through the CLI's own validator — confirmed against the shipped Claude Code binary (2.1.259) rather than documentation.
- **Every mechanical gate in the repo is green**: 7,475/7,475 unit tests, both typecheck projects, ESLint, both generator `--check`s, all three vendored artefact mirrors byte-identical to source, four changed e2e specs re-run by hand and passing.
- **Casting and type-safety discipline held** across the diff: zero `as unknown as`, zero non-null matrix-lookup assertions, the two casts present both fall inside CLAUDE.md's own stated exceptions (a parse boundary, a documented error-path case).

## 5. Where the guidance itself is in tension

**Verification instruction vs. deterministic gate (Rule 5 vs. the brief's over-verification principle).** The baseline kept one sentence — "re-read a file after editing it... before reporting the work done." The brief says explicit verification wastes turns on a model that doesn't need it; the session's own gate is the mechanism meant to replace exactly this. But the gate provably cannot see this failure mode: it typechecks and lints, and a no-op edit that leaves prior, valid content in place typechecks and lints clean — reproduced directly (`gate exit=0` on a `.ts` file whose edit never landed). So the sentence and the gate are not substitutes on this one failure class; they're complementary, and prompt-bible's own Technique #11 says so. What's unresolved is whether that complementarity is worth 348 bytes × 18 agents forever, or whether the second half of the sentence (naming what would catch a violation) is the only part worth keeping and the mechanical read-back half should go to the hook after all.

**Progressive disclosure vs. core slimming, applied to the same bytes.** Rule 2 asks for both "reduce by up to 80%" and "lazy-load via Skill or sub-file." The session did the first and not the second for the five deleted methodology partials — the content is gone, not relocated, and nothing in the rendered baseline points anywhere. That's a legitimate reading of "slimming" but a failure of "disclosure" on the same material, and the two halves of one rule pulled toward opposite outputs: a pointer costs bytes the slimming pass was trying to remove.

**Rule 1's "assume advanced model reasoning" vs. Rule 5's gate needing something to react to.** The 14 `(You MUST run tests...)` parentheticals and 8 self-attestation checklist rows are exactly the micromanagement Rule 1 wants gone — but they're also the _only_ thing in the prompt telling an agent to run tests at all, since the gate never got the `test` script added. Deleting the prose without extending the gate leaves nothing enforcing test execution; extending the gate without deleting the prose leaves the redundancy Rule 1 was written against. The two moves are coupled and the session did neither half completely.

**"Researched suggestions are to be BUILT, not audited for staleness" vs. the documentation-bible's re-derivation contract.** The ruling pushes toward implementing the brief's principles quickly and broadly; the bible demands every claim in a touched document be re-verified against source before the `last_validated` stamp moves. This session did the first at some expense of the second — real content landed (the gate, the isolation/experimental keys, the rewritten summoners) faster than the surrounding documentation could be swept for consistency, which is most of why section 4's important-severity list exists. Building fast and re-deriving fully are in tension whenever a change touches more surface than one session can fully sweep, and this session is a clean example of picking the first at a measurable cost to the second.

**Single scope decision, two audiences.** The session scoped itself to "the two summoners, the shared template, and the two standards documents" (recorded explicitly in the plan). Rule 1 and the brief's principles are written as claims about "generated system prompts" — a set of 18, not 2. A narrow, honestly-scoped session and a broadly-stated rule will always produce this exact shape of gap; the fix isn't in this session's work, it's in whether future briefs state "the two summoners" or "all agents" up front so the scoreboard doesn't have to infer which was meant.
